# Narrative reference — how to tell a share diagnosis

Derived from a client-ready deliverable that told the Schylling story well. This is the structure/voice standard for the artifact. The four lanes are the analytical engine; this is how the result gets *told*. The rule of thumb: a reader who skims only the headline, the section leads, and the recommendations should get the entire argument.

## The shape

**Verdict headline.** The `<h1>` is a claim: "Category discovery is broken." Not "Share diagnosis — [brand]." The title is the conclusion, stated flatly.

**Hero argument (2–3 sentences).** Argue the whole case immediately, leading with the reframe. The strongest pattern is the paradox-in-numbers:

> "Schylling grew revenue +74% YoY. The category grew +323%. That gap — not a competitor — is the diagnosis. Three structural problems compounded to produce a −7.8pp share loss."

Note what this does: it kills the obvious wrong read ("a competitor beat us"), names the real one, and previews the structure — all before any chart.

## Named failures, not a lane grid

Synthesize the drivers into **2–3 named failures**, each a short phrase a reader will remember and a one-line thesis:

- *Category SERP collapse* — organic visibility on generic terms fell off a cliff.
- *Branded-term infiltration* — competitors/generics now rank on the brand's own name.
- *Launch-execution gap* — new SKUs don't convert to reviews, so they never rank.

Lead the diagnosis section with a claim about how they relate: "The share loss is not one event. These three problems are reinforcing each other — and all three need separate responses on different timelines." The explicit reframe is the move that makes it read as insight: *"What looks like competitive displacement is actually a product-expansion asymmetry."*

## Section pattern (repeat per failure)

- **Kicker** (mono, uppercase): `02 / Share of voice`
- **Title** (phrase): "The SERP collapse in detail"
- **Lead** (a claim, full sentence): "The two keyword types tell completely different stories — and the difference is the whole diagnosis."
- **Callout** carrying meaning, colored by severity: *"The critical signal: category organic SOV has been collapsing since February — from 64% to 10% in four months, while sponsored share sat near zero the entire window."*
- **Chart card**: title + a **subtitle that states the takeaway**, not axis names. The chart illustrates the sentence; it doesn't replace it.

Numbers live inside sentences ("from 64% to 10% in four months"), not in naked tables above the fold.

## Sizing = three horizons

Tell dollars as time, each a sentence:
- **Realized miss** — what's already gone (annualized run-rate).
- **Forward exposure** — what's at risk next quarter if nothing changes (range when noisy).
- **Recoverable** — what a spend/pricing decision recovers near-term.

## Focus areas = where to concentrate

The payoff section. Present as **areas to focus**, ordered by leverage — highest-leverage, highest-confidence first. Each with: the specific focus, an estimated recovery/impact, and a watch-metric. Distinguish the quicker recapture (open sponsored spend on category terms) from the deeper structural fix (rebuild launch velocity) in leverage terms, not on a clock. Do not attach timeframes, deadlines, or sequencing language. Example focus areas:

1. Defend the brand on branded terms — *estimated recovery: $X.* Watch: branded organic SOV.
2. Open sponsored spend on category terms — quick recapture, low commitment. Watch: category sponsored share.
3. Rebuild launch velocity — the structural root cause; deeper fix. Watch: new-SKU review accrual.
4. Monitor the generic conquest — a watch item, not yet an area to act on. Watch: Generic category organic SOV.

## Voice and visual

- Prose carries the argument; dense tables go to an evidence appendix at the end.
- Every chart has a takeaway subtitle; callouts state meaning, not restated numbers.
- Light editorial theme, four-step text hierarchy, color only on signal, sans for prose + mono for kickers/data. Palette and chart templates come from `jungle-scout-visualizer`; the editorial rules are in SKILL.md Output → House style.
- The coverage-by-lane table (the completeness check) lives in the appendix, not the top — blind spots stay visible without leading the story.
